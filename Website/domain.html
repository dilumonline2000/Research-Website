§ 01 Literature survey

Mature fragments, no whole.

Global scholarly output grew from roughly 2.1 million articles in 2010 to more than 3.6 million in 2022. Each research-support task has its own literature, but no existing tool connects them.

A

Citation generation, gap analysis & mind mapping

Citation research has focused on classifying citation contexts rather than producing correctly formatted references. CERMINE extracts metadata but cannot render a target style. LLM agents such as ResearchAgent generate new ideas but do not map what a corpus leaves uncovered, and model-generated ideas are judged more novel but less feasible. Mind maps are produced as standalone artefacts, disconnected from gap analysis.

Iqbal et al. · CERMINE · ResearchAgent · Si et al. · ProposaGen · MindMap (KG prompting)
B

Collaboration & recommendation

Academic recommenders mostly recommend papers rather than people, and supervisor allocation is rarely modelled. When it is, it relies on keyword overlap, which fails when a student and a supervisor describe the same problem in different words. Sentence-BERT solves that with semantic embeddings. General BERT sentiment classifiers mislabel constructive academic critique as negative.

Chen & Chen · Sentence-BERT · Johnson et al. · BERT
C

Document analysis & integrity

Repositories rely on inconsistent manually assigned keywords. SciBERT and BERTopic improve scientific classification, and T5 and BART produce readable abstractive summaries. Plagiarism detectors handle verbatim copying well but struggle with paraphrase, so hybrid lexical and semantic similarity performs better. Corpus-level trend views are rarely offered.

SciBERT · BERTopic · T5 · BART · Foltýnek et al. · Thompson et al.
D

Research analytics

Forecasting research trends is a time-series problem with irregular sampling and structural breaks. ARIMA and Prophet each address part of it. Quality assessment still relies on citation counts, which lag too long to be useful for student projects, or on manual rubrics applied inconsistently.

Box–Jenkins ARIMA · Prophet (Taylor & Letham) · Aksnes et al.
§ 02 Research gap

Six gaps, one root cause.

The literature review identified six gaps. G1 drives most of the others, because when the toolchain is fragmented, no component can use what another has produced.

G1 · ROOT GAP

No platform unifies citation support, collaboration matching, document analysis and research analytics over a shared, institution-specific data substrate.

G2

Reference managers still need manual entry, and metadata extractors do not produce formatted output.

G3

Idea generation exists, but systematic coverage analysis of a corpus, and visualising it, do not.

G4

Supervisor allocation is manual or keyword-based, and no sentiment model is tuned to academic critique.

G5

Similarity checkers give a per-document score, not an institution-wide view of overlap trends.

G6

No consolidated corpus supports trend forecasting or transparent quality assessment.

ROOT CAUSES

Fragmented tooling · generic, uncitable AI evidence · missing people-matching · unanalysable repositories · no institutional foresight.

§ 03 Research problem

The question.

University students and institutions lack an integrated, explainable research-support system that grounds its recommendations in the institution's own research output.

Students move between disconnected tools for referencing, justifying a topic, choosing a supervisor, checking overlap and assessing quality. Meanwhile the institution cannot analyse its research output as a corpus. The result is lost time, avoidable citation and integrity errors, poorly matched supervision and decisions made without evidence.

§ 04 Research objectives

From topic selection to proposal.

Main aim: design, implement and evaluate an integrated, AI-powered research assistant and collaboration platform that supports university students from topic selection to proposal submission, grounded in the institution's own research output.

GO1

One authenticated platform

A single web platform gives access to every research-support feature.

GO2

Shared embedded corpus

Build and maintain one institutional corpus used by every component.

GO3

Grounded recommendations

Gaps, proposals, related papers and matches all cite identifiable SLIIT research.

GO4

Accuracy targets

Meet the component-level accuracy targets set for the core models.

GO5

Live validation

Validate every component through live functional tests signed off by the supervisor.

GO6

Continuous deployment

Deploy continuously on cloud infrastructure using CPU-only inference.

§ 05 Methodology

Design Science Research, in sprints.

The platform followed Design Science Research (Hevner et al.) in iterative two-week Scrum sprints. Each module was evaluated on its own before integration.

Problem identification

Literature survey, stakeholder needs, supervisor discussions.

Artefact design

Interface-first architecture, data contracts, shared schema.

Development

Four FastAPI services, a gateway and a Next.js client, built in a monorepo.

Evaluation

Held-out metrics, benchmarks, back-tests and 52 live test cases.

System architecture

Four layers. The gateway verifies Supabase JWTs, applies rate limits and proxies requests to each module. Every service falls back through three tiers (local model → Gemini → deterministic heuristic), so the product never fails outright.

M1 · METHOD

Deterministic regex citation engine (APA 7 / IEEE) with CrossRef lookup; SBERT retrieval over gap statements with multi-dimensional ranking; retrieval-grounded proposal composition; concept mind maps.

M2 · METHOD

SBERT bi-encoder fine-tuned with contrastive loss on 147 labelled pairs. Score = 0.70 × similarity + 0.30 × availability, with a template explanation for each match.

M3 · METHOD

TF-IDF one-vs-rest multi-label categorisation; extractive summarisation with centroid scoring and MMR; overlap = 75% SBERT + 25% 4-gram Jaccard.

M4 · METHOD

Per-topic ARIMA(1,1,1) with 95% intervals; XGBoost regressors blended with heuristics; success = 40% XGBoost + 60% heuristic.

Research-quality composite score (from the research paper):

Q = 0.30·Soriginality + 0.25·Scitation + 0.25·Smethod + 0.20·Sclarity
§ 06 Results

Measured against pre-set targets.

Every success threshold was fixed in the proposals, before implementation started. Of the 52 live validation cases, 36 passed, 9 were partial, 6 failed and 1 was blocked. None of the failures was architectural.

§ 07 Technologies used

CPU-only, explainable by design.
